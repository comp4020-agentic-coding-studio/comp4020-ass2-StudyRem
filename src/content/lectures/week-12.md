No new theory this week. Every idea the Capstone needs --- faucets and sinks,
market design, cost-effectiveness ratios, time-gating, mechanism design ---
was introduced somewhere in Weeks 1 through 11. This lecture spends its time
on two or three in-progress interventions, worked through out loud, so
everyone can see what a marker is actually going to look for.

## What a strong intervention argument looks like

- names the *specific* real economy (EVE, WoW or RuneScape) and the
  *specific* documented problem, not a generic "inflation is bad"
- picks one concrete mechanism from the semester --- a new sink, a changed
  matching rule, a mechanism-design payment change --- rather than several
  vague ones at once
- says what the intervention would cost the game to implement in player
  goodwill or complexity, because every real intervention this course has
  studied cost something

## What sinks a mark

Proposing a fix for a problem that doesn't have documented evidence behind
it, or one so large ("redesign the whole economy") that it can't be argued
for with the tools this course actually taught.
