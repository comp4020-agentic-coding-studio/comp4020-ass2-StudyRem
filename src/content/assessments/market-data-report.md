## The brief

> Read a real economy's numbers the way its designers have to, and say what
> they mean.

You'll work from a real, publicly published data source on a virtual
economy --- most likely EVE Online's Monthly Economic Reports, which is
what Week 4's lecture and deck cover in depth, though you may substitute
another published source with the convenor's agreement. The vocabulary
this report actually asks you to apply --- faucets, sinks, and where they're
out of balance --- is Week 2's, not Week 3's: Market Data Lab gave you
practice spotting a *different* kind of mismatch (a traded price diverging
from its listed guide price), a related but distinct skill worth keeping
separate in your head from the faucet/sink imbalance this report is
actually marked on.

A strong report doesn't just describe the data --- it identifies the
mechanisms producing it. Two numbers moving together is an observation.
Naming the faucet creating currency and the sink removing it, and pointing
at where they're mismatched, is analysis.

## What you submit

A short written report (roughly 800--1200 words, or equivalent in
figures-plus-commentary) that:

- names at least two faucets and two sinks visible in your chosen data
  source, with a citation to where in the source you found them
- identifies one specific point of imbalance --- a trend, a spike, a
  divergence --- and explains what it suggests about the economy's health
- is written for a reader who has not opened the source themselves

Cite your data source by name and link. If you use EVE Online's Monthly
Economic Reports, [the report hub is here](https://www.eveonline.com/news/t/monthly-economic-reports).
