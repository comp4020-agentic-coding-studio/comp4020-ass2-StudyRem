## The brief

> Every player-run market eventually meets someone willing to break it on
> purpose. Explain how, and what would have stopped them.

Week 8's lecture covered the Guiding Hand Social Club heist in EVE
Online --- a real, decade-old incident where a ten-month espionage
operation ended in the destruction of a player [corporation](/glossary/#corporation)'s assets worth
tens of billions of [ISK](/glossary/#isk), reported at the time as somewhere north of
$16,000 in real-world value. That's one option. Comparative Market
Teardown (Week 9) will have had you dig into specific mechanics across
EVE Online, World of Warcraft, and Old School RuneScape; you may use that
research to choose a different documented incident from any of the
course's three case-study games, or another virtual economy, provided it
is real and citable.

The essay is not a retelling. A market-manipulation story is interesting;
the mechanism behind it is what this course has spent nine weeks giving
you the tools to name.

## What you submit

A single essay (roughly 1200--1800 words) that:

- identifies the incident by name, date (where known), and source
- explains the specific market or economic mechanism it exploited ---
  what rule, gap, or incentive made the exploit possible
- proposes one plausible design change that would have closed that gap,
  reasoned from the course's mechanism-design and market-design material

This is marked holistically rather than against a weighted checklist:
the mark reflects the essay as a whole, not a sum of separately-scored
parts.
