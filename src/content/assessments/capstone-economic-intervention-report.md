## The brief

> No one can design an economy from a blank page --- there's no context to
> be right or wrong about. But every real economy already has a problem
> worth fixing, and that's a brief with teeth.

This capstone deliberately isn't "design your own virtual economy." You
will pick one real, documented economy --- EVE Online is the natural
choice given the semester's throughline, but World of Warcraft or Old
School RuneScape are both acceptable if that's what you went deep on in
Week 9's comparative teardown. From that economy, you'll identify one
specific, real problem: an inflation spiral visible in published data, a
market that's gone dead or illiquid, a faucet being exploited at scale,
or a comparable documented issue. You then propose one intervention ---
a policy, a market mechanism, or a numeric rebalance --- scoped tightly
enough to address that one problem, and justified with the tools this
course has built up: faucet/sink analysis, cost-effectiveness ratios,
market microstructure, and mechanism design.

This is a bigger, semester-integrating version of Balance Patch Notes
(Week 6), not a repeat of it: Balance Patch Notes rebalanced one dataset
handed to you in the room, over a single session. Here, you find the
problem yourself, in a real published economy, and the intervention has
to hold up against a real system's actual scale and history --- not a
simplified in-class dataset.

## What you submit

1. A written report (roughly 2000--3000 words) that:
   - names the real economy and cites the specific, verifiable problem
     within it
   - proposes one scoped intervention addressing that problem
   - includes at least one worked numerical model supporting the
     intervention's expected effect (a ratio, a projection, or a
     before/after estimate, shown with its working)
   - acknowledges at least one tradeoff or failure mode of your own
     proposal
2. A timed pitch (under three minutes) delivered in the Week 12 Capstone
   Pitch Rehearsal session, covering the economy, the problem, and the
   intervention.

## How it's marked

- **Design coherence (40%)** --- the intervention is scoped to the named
  problem, internally consistent, and doesn't quietly redesign the whole
  economy to solve a narrow issue.
- **Numerical modeling (30%)** --- the worked model is correct, shown with
  its working, and actually supports the claimed effect.
- **Pitch (30%)** --- the timed pitch is clear, fits its time limit, and a
  listener who hasn't read the report can follow the argument.
