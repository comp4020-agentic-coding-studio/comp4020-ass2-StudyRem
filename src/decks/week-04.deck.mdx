# The EVE Online Economy

Reading a decade of Monthly Economic Reports

---

## Why EVE, specifically

- one server, one currency, two decades running
- CCP Games has published a **Monthly Economic Report** (MER) for years —
  faucets, sinks, prices, destruction, all of it
- no other case study in this course has anything close to that public a
  record

---

## What's in a MER

- **money supply** — total ISK in player hands, over time
- **production & mining value** — what's being made, and from what
- **destruction value** — what got blown up, priced at replacement cost
- **Active ISK Delta** — net faucet minus sink, for players active that
  month specifically

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> Destruction value counts as an economic indicator because in EVE, blowing
> things up is the dominant currency sink — not an accident of combat, a
> design decision.

---

## Faucets, in this economy

- mission and bounty payouts
- mining and production income
- NPC buy orders on raw materials

Every one of these is a designer-controlled tap. None of them are "natural."

---

## Sinks, in this economy

- ship and module loss — the big one
- clone and implant costs after a death
- market broker fees and sales tax
- skill injectors, which convert real money into a currency sink directly

---

## Reading the Active ISK Delta

- spikes after every expansion — new and returning players, faucets running
  before sinks catch up
- a sustained positive delta with no expansion nearby is the signal a
  designer reads as "something's over-paying"
- CCP has, more than once, nerfed a payout curve in direct response to
  exactly this signal

---

## Worked example: a capital ship loss

- a single capital-ship loss can represent billions of ISK removed from the
  economy in one destruction event
- the pilot's *insurance* payout is itself a faucet, partially offsetting
  the sink — the two numbers appear in the same report, on purpose
- reading only the destruction total without the insurance faucet
  overstates how much the economy actually lost

---

## Regional markets aren't one market

- prices for the same mineral can differ meaningfully between regions,
  especially null-sec vs. safe empire space
- that gap *is* the logistics problem from Week 7 — someone has to move the
  goods, at a risk, to close it
- the MER breaks activity down by region for exactly this reason

---

## What the reports can't tell you

- **who** did something, only **how much** — MERs are aggregate, not
  forensic
- they can't distinguish "the economy is healthy" from "the economy is
  healthy for whoever already has the most"
- reading them is necessary, not sufficient — Week 8's case studies are
  what the aggregate numbers miss

---

## Before the assessment

The Market Data Report asks you to read a MER you haven't seen in class and
find where the story the numbers tell surprised you.
